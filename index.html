Important Bioinformatics Tools Should Be Well Maintained

We aim to maintain and modernize widely used bioinformatics toolkits by building a thriving community rooted in collaborative development, rigorous validation, transparent AI governance, and lasting recognition of contributors.

Our Vision

Many bioinformatics pipelines remain foundational to scientific research but receive less maintenance over time as developers move on or funding ends. If you maintain a widely used pipeline and would welcome help sustaining it, we invite you to contact us. We will make every effort to identify resources and collaborators to support its continued maintenance. We understand that, for many developers, these projects represent years of dedication and personal commitment. We will treat them with care and respect, and we welcome the original developers’ continued involvement, feedback, and oversight.

Although AI can assist with software development, people remain at the heart of maintaining bioinformatics pipelines. Every contribution deserves recognition. We are developing a contribution tracking and attribution system to acknowledge everyone who helps improve the community’s software, whether by identifying a small bug in a bioinformatics pipeline or making it just 1% faster. Under traditional academic reward systems, these small but valuable contributions often go unrecognized and rarely lead to publications or research funding. We want to create a lasting record of these efforts and ensure that the people behind them receive credit. As resources become available, we also hope to give back to the community and provide meaningful support to its contributors.

As more developers use AI to optimize code, these changes require careful oversight to assess whether they are safe to use, preserve correctness, and appropriately respect and credit the original authors. In maintaining these bioinformatics pipelines, we will strive to uphold these principles through thorough human code review and an immutable record of contributions across each pipeline’s development history. Once a contribution is incorporated into a release, its attribution will remain part of the contribution history accompanying every subsequent release, ensuring lasting recognition for the contributor.

Our Approach

We use AI to help refactor existing bioinformatics code and improve its documentation, making complex codebases easier to read, understand, and maintain over time. Because accuracy and reproducibility are essential in bioinformatics, all AI-generated code will undergo thorough human review. We will also conduct extensive regression testing across diverse datasets to verify consistency with the original implementations and preserve the accuracy and reproducibility of their results.

We improve implementation efficiency through both AI-assisted development and manual optimization. Many existing bioinformatics tools offer substantial opportunities for performance improvements, particularly as advances in modern hardware create new ways to accelerate computation. These improvements can reduce computational resource requirements and execution time.

Our team includes several researchers with backgrounds in theoretical computer science, a field that has long contributed foundational ideas to bioinformatics. Examples include the FM-index used in BWA, suffix arrays used in STAR, network flow algorithms used in StringTie, and dynamic programming algorithms used throughout sequence analysis. Building on this foundation, we investigate opportunities to improve the algorithms underlying widely used bioinformatics tools. We will validate new algorithms against the original implementations to verify output equivalence under the same inputs and settings.

We integrate bioinformatics tools into interoperable workflows by standardizing their interfaces and harmonizing input, output, and intermediate data formats. Our goal is to make these tools easier to invoke programmatically and combine into automated analyses. Structured descriptions of tool capabilities, parameters, and data requirements will also help AI systems select appropriate tools, invoke them correctly, and assemble reproducible workflows.
